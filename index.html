🧬 Cancer Evolution Simulator

Explore how mutations, growth rates and chemotherapy can affect cancer cell populations over time.

How does it work?

Cancer cells can acquire mutations as they divide. These mutations can create different groups of cancer cells, called clones.

In this simulation, the original cancer cells are shown in green. A mutation can create a new clone, shown in blue.

The blue clone has a slightly higher growth rate than the original clone. This means the two clones can grow at different speeds.

Chemotherapy is introduced once during the simulation. It reduces both clones, after which the surviving cells can continue growing.

ℹ️ Model Note

This is a simplified educational model rather than a realistic model of cancer growth.

The green population represents the original cancer clone. The blue population represents a clone that arose after an additional mutation.

The mutation is modelled as giving the blue clone a higher growth rate. It does not mean that the blue cells are automatically more cancerous or chemotherapy-resistant.

Chemotherapy is modelled as a single treatment that removes a proportion of both clones. The surviving cells can then continue to grow.
